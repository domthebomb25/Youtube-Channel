**Plain narration:**
> Have you ever noticed the McDonald's ice cream machine is always broken? It's not your imagination. And the reason is wild. Every night, the machine runs a heat-cleaning cycle to kill bacteria. It can take up to four hours. If anything goes wrong, it locks itself… and shows error codes workers can't make sense of. So the restaurant has to call a technician approved by the company that makes the machine. Then a tiny startup built a gadget that decoded the errors. Owners loved it. McDonald's told franchise owners to stop using it. The startup sued for nine hundred million dollars. Even the FTC started asking questions. In 2024, the government finally made it legal for outside repair shops to unlock these machines. So next time it's "broken"… it might just be cleaning… or still waiting on the repair guy.

## Fact check (sources)
- Nightly heat-cleaning cycle that can take up to 4 hours; a failed cycle keeps the machine down until a technician fixes it — WSJ, Sept 2021 (via Boston Globe / NBC). ✅
- Startup Kytch built a device that reads the Taylor machine's error data; McDonald's told franchisees to remove it in Nov 2020 (citing safety and warranty). ✅
- Kytch sued McDonald's for $900 million (Delaware federal court). Settlement talks reported Nov 2024 — outcome not confirmed, so the script doesn't claim a result. ✅
- FTC sent letters to franchisees in summer 2021 asking about the machines ("preliminary" inquiry). ✅
- Oct 2024: U.S. Copyright Office granted a narrow exemption letting third parties get past the machines' digital locks to repair them. ✅
- Not claimed (unconfirmed): exact repair prices, that breakdowns are intentional.
