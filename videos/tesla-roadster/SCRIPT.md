**VO:** "On Thursday, October 15th, at a SpaceX facility in Texas, Tesla finally showed the new Roadster, nearly nine years after it was pitched in 2017. ## Part 4 — How it flies
**VO:** "So how does it fly? Tesla says it's cold-gas thrusters, the same idea SpaceX uses on its rockets. Compressed air from big carbon-fiber tanks behind the seats shoots out of the nozzles. There's a yoke wheel. The Rocket is a one-seater, while the Performance and Thrust are two-seaters. And only the top trim gets the full thruster setup. The base Performance version reportedly has none."

## Part 5 — The pivot
**VO:** "But how it flies is the easy question. The better question is why build a car that flies for a few seconds? That answer is right on Tesla's own website."

## Part 6 — The money
**VO:** "The Performance model costs fifty thousand dollars per reservation. Five thousand up front, then a forty five thousand dollar wire within ten days. That's the road ready one. The Thrust model costs two hundred and fifty thousand dollars per reservation. That's the limited one that does 0-60 in 0.81 seconds. And the Rocket? Invite only. You can't reserve it even if you have the money. Now here's the kicker. Production doesn't start until 2028. So you pay TODAY, you MIGHT get a car in TWO YEARS. Now let's do the math. Say two thousand people reserve the Performance. That's one hundred million dollars. Say one thousand die-hard fans reserve the Thrust. That's two hundred and fifty million. Three hundred and fifty million dollars. If that were real, Tesla would be sitting on that much of its customers' money, possibly interest free, for a car that doesn't exist yet. That's what I mean by printing money."

## Part 7 — They've done this before
**VO:** "No bank loan. No interest. No giving up Tesla stock. Just fans giving Tesla a free loan for 2 years. And they've done this before. In 2017, Tesla took similar fifty and two hundred fifty thousand dollar deposits for the Roadster promise, with production due in 2020. Nearly nine years later, some people are still waiting. Sam Altman reserved back in 2018. He tried to cancel last year, had trouble doing it, and it turned into an online feud with Musk. Accountants call this business strategy a float."

## Part 8 — Why now
**VO:** "So why fly it this week? Tesla's sales have been under pressure. BYD has been outselling Tesla in Europe, although its numbers include plug-in hybrids. And Tesla's third-quarter deliveries, about four hundred eighty-six thousand, beat analyst expectations, but were still down two percent from a year ago. When your everyday cars aren't exciting anyone, you build one that makes every news channel say 'Tesla is the future again.' That's called a halo product. Ferrari does it. Almost nobody buys the flagship, but it makes the normal cars feel special. Most people will never own the Rocket. But now the car in your driveway feels like it comes from the rocket company."

## Part 9 — The answer and the question
**VO:** "So, can Tesla's new car fly? Technically, yes. Real SpaceX thrusters, real flight, and real results. But the flight lasts seconds. The deposits last years. And if my math is even close, it just printed them hundreds of millions of dollars in one night. So, would you put fifty thousand dollars down for a car you might have to wait years for?
