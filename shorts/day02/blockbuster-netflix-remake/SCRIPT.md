## Facts and sources (checked Oct 14 2026)
- $50M offer in 2000, Blockbuster declined; "struggling not to laugh" is Marc Randolph's account (book That Will Never Work): CNBC 2020-09-22, Fortune 2023-04-14, InsideHook, Inc. excerpt.
- Nearly $800M late fees = 16% of revenue in 2000: NBC News (Hubris - and late fees - doomed Blockbuster), Business Insider. Secondary reporting, not found in Blockbuster's own filings.
- $134M late fees (3% of revenue) shortly before bankruptcy: NBC News. Year not stated precisely, so the line is undated.
- 9,094 stores / 84,300 staff in 2004: Wikipedia; Sound & Vision says "over 9,000".
- Late fees ended as of Jan 1 2005: CBS News / Newsweek.
- Chapter 11 in September 2010: Dish buy articles (Boulder Weekly, 9to5mac, TechCrunch).
- Netflix market cap about $300-345B in mid-2026 (247WallSt, FinHacker, Bullfincher), so "hundreds of billions".
- Dropped on purpose: 2004 $1.2B loss (mostly non-cash impairments, misleading), $145M vs $200M late-fee cost (sources conflict), "$ lost per day" (no honest figure).
