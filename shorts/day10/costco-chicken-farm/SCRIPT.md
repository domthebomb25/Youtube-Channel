# Costco built a chicken farm to keep the $4.99 chicken

**Title:** Costco Built a Chicken Farm to Keep Its Chicken $4.99?
**Payoff hook (option A):** COSTCO BUILT ITS OWN CHICKEN FARM… TO KEEP THE $4.99 PRICE
**Format:** You're the company + loop. ~45 s, voice Benji, no music, no end text.

## Narration (numbers spelled out for alignment)
1. Costco built its own chicken farm… just to keep this chicken at four ninety-nine.
2. Say you run Costco. Your rotisserie chicken costs four ninety-nine, and it's the reason people walk in.
3. But chicken costs more every year.
4. Raise the price, and shoppers notice. Keep it, and you lose money.
5. Costco's own finance chief admitted keeping it low costs the company millions.
6. So Costco kept it. Four ninety-nine, since two thousand nine.
7. But it didn't stop at eating the loss. In twenty sixteen, Costco started its own poultry company.
8. It built a farm and a processing plant in Nebraska.
9. Now Costco controls part of the chicken supply, from the egg to the store, which keeps its costs down.
10. Not every chicken, though. Costco still buys from other suppliers too.
11. And it works. Costco sells over a hundred million of these every year.
12. Because shoppers who come for a cheap chicken leave with a full cart.
13. And that's why Costco built its own chicken farm.   (loops into line 1)

## Verified facts (WebSearch Oct 19 2026)
- $4.99 since 2009 (briefly raised $1 in 2008, then cut back). Loss leader. Former CFO said on a 2015 earnings call that holding the price costs multiple millions; exact loss NOT public (do not quote $30-40M, secondhand).
- 2016: Costco created Lincoln Premium Poultry; Nebraska farm + Fremont plant. Covers only PART of supply; Costco still buys from other suppliers.
- Volume: 100M+ per year (sources vary: 106M over a decade, 137M in 2023).
